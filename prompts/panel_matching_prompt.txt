TASK:
Match the gene-disease association at the end to every panel it belongs on. Its disease and its summary both come from the association's papers: the disease names the disorder and lists its reported features, and the summary describes the affected individuals, how many have each feature, and the evidence.

A clinician orders a panel when a patient's features raise the suspicion of a disorder the panel covers, often before the full picture is clear. A gene missing from a panel can mean a missed diagnosis, while a gene on a panel it does not belong on costs curators' review and dilutes the panel. An association belongs on a panel when a clinician seeing patients like these would order it.

MATCHING PRINCIPLES:
1. The panel's relevant disorders, disease group and description define its scope, including any exclusions the description makes.
2. Judge by the features the papers report, not by what is known about the disease from elsewhere, and read clinical terms for their meaning.
3. The mode of inheritance, age of onset and other qualifiers in a panel's name or description must agree with the association's.
4. Each rationale names the reported features that place the association on the panel, with how many individuals have them.
