TASK:
Match the gene-disease association at the end to every panel it belongs on. Its disease and its summary both come from the association's papers: the disease names the disorder and lists its reported features, and the summary describes the affected individuals, how many have each feature, and the evidence. After them, each of these papers is shown as extracted from its full text, with every disease it reports for the association's gene. A paper may also report other diseases of the gene. A disease belongs to this association when it is the same disorder with the same mode of inheritance, whatever the paper calls it. Draw the reported features only from the diseases that belong, and do not match a panel because of a disease that does not. Papers often report the same individuals again, so take how many individuals have a feature from the summary or from a single paper, never by adding up papers.

An association belongs on a panel when its affected individuals have the disorder the panel covers, as the panel defines it: the papers say so, or report the findings that define it. The disorder may be part of a wider syndrome and need not occur in every affected individual: the association belongs when individuals the papers report have the disorder itself, even when it is variable in the disease as a whole. A disease that shares only some of the disorder's features, a finding that resembles it, or an underlying mechanism with it does not belong on the panel. When a panel's description defines its disorder by its primary pathology or cause, findings that arise secondarily from another primary disease do not make the individuals have that disorder, however consistently they occur. Apply these criteria from scratch, as if the panels were being built today: ignore which genes or diseases any panel, in PanelApp or elsewhere, currently lists, and do not recall or check whether the gene is on one, since panels hold genes added under other criteria. When it is doubtful whether the individuals have a panel's disorder, do not match that panel.

MATCHING PRINCIPLES:
1. The panel's relevant disorders, disease group and description define its scope, including any exclusions the description makes.
2. Judge by the features the papers report, not by what is known about the disease from elsewhere, and read clinical terms for their meaning.
3. The mode of inheritance, age of onset and other qualifiers in a panel's name or description must agree with the association's.
4. Each rationale gives the papers' statement that the individuals have the panel's disorder, or the reported findings that define it, with how many individuals have them.
